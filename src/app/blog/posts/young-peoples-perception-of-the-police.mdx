## Breaking Barriers Through Design Thinking 🤝

In May 2024 I took part in a design workshop at Birmingham Metropolitan College focused on police–youth relations. The idea was simple in theory and difficult in practice: get young people and police officers in the same room and work on digital ideas that might actually help.

### Who Was in the Room 👥

**16–19-year-old students** from Birmingham worked alongside **20 West Midlands Police officers**. Designers from Experience Haus facilitated, **AXON** sponsored the event, and mentors from local and national charities kept the brief grounded in what communities actually need.

### The Brief 📝

The aim was to **improve how young people and the police interact**, using digital tools where they made sense. That meant listening properly, not jumping to a flashy app for the sake of it.

### What We Designed 🧩

Two concepts came out of the sessions:

#### 🚨 Rights & Responsibilities App
A mobile app to support young people during Stop & Search:
- Clear information on rights and responsibilities
- Guidance during interactions with police
- Something to refer to when you are under pressure, not only afterwards

#### 🗺️ Community Safety Hub
A website acting as a local hub:
- Directory of activities and opportunities
- Crime awareness mapping
- Updates on community safety issues
- A route into positive engagement, not only warnings

### Beyond One Workshop 🌍

This Birmingham session was part of a **national series** of similar events. The ideas from each workshop are being developed into real products, not left as slide decks.

What stood out to me was how seriously the young participants took it when they were given time, structure, and adults who would listen. You could see people realise their input might actually change how things work locally.

### Closing Thought 💬

Good community relations start with honest conversation and shared problem-solving. This workshop was one example of that: mixed groups, design methods, and a commitment to build something useful afterwards.
