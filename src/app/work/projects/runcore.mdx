RunCore started as a simple idea: I wanted a running tool that actually feels like mine. I didn’t want to depend on a subscription, an external API, or a platform that keeps my training data somewhere else. I wanted something lightweight, private, and useful enough to fit into my real routine.

So I built RunCore around two things I care about most:

- **My data stays on my device.**
- **My insights feel personal, immediate, and useful.**

The setup is deliberately simple. I use Apple Shortcuts to record the details of each run, and Apple Intelligence to analyse that information locally on the device. Nothing gets sent away for processing, and I keep full control over the workflow.

---

## How it works

### 1. I log the run with a Shortcut

After each workout, I trigger a Shortcut that captures the details I care about most:

- distance
- duration
- pace
- heart rate
- cadence
- conditions
- effort

That small bit of structure keeps the whole system predictable. I don’t need a big ecosystem around it, and I’m not relying on fragile integrations or hidden platform rules.

### 2. Apple Intelligence analyses it locally

Once the run data is in, Apple Intelligence processes it on-device. That’s the heart of the privacy model here: the analysis happens locally, without sending my workout information to external servers.

From there, it turns raw numbers into more useful outputs, such as:

- performance summaries
- training load estimates
- pacing trends
- recovery suggestions
- coaching notes

It’s the kind of feedback I actually want to read after a run, without having to chase it through a third-party dashboard.

### 3. I get a readable RunCore report

The Shortcut returns a clean report with the main takeaways from the session. It’s fast, consistent, and designed to help me understand what happened in the run without needing a full fitness platform around it.

---

## Why I built it this way

The fitness ecosystem is increasingly messy. Strava has changed its API direction, Apple has restricted some automated HealthKit exports, and a lot of apps are built around cloud processing and subscriptions. I wanted something simpler.

I wanted a tool that was:

- private
- local
- personal
- easy to understand
- fully under my control

RunCore avoids the parts I don’t want in my workflow:

- API limitations
- subscription walls
- cloud storage
- dependency on external services

And it leans into the parts that matter to me:

- local processing
- minimal manual input
- privacy-first design
- Apple-native automation

---

## Still in progress

RunCore is close to a first full release, but I’m still refining a few parts of it:

- the Shortcut input flow
- the Apple Intelligence prompts
- the format of the final report
- a few possible integrations with my personal dashboard

The goal is a running analyst that feels effortless to use, useful enough to trust, and completely aligned with how I want to work with my own data.
